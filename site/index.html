Open-source Mujina mining daemon onto Intel Blockscale BZM2 silicon. Validation is gated: vendor RDS characterization (RDS-*), then Mujina bring-up (MJ-*). Nothing hashes under Mujina control until the thermal interlock is in place.

Port thesis

The driver was written and reviewed against documentation; it had never been run against silicon. This site tracks the bring-up on an RDS 2.0 control board: what we tried, what the harness got wrong, what the daemon got wrong, and which cases still gate hashing.

It is a lab notebook for a firmware port — not a throughput leaderboard, not a storage benchmark, and not a product launch page.

Current phase

Vendor stack characterized for everything short of sustained hashing. Mujina attaches to a real chain and reads it. The remaining gap before we drive the hardware ourselves is an interlock that refuses to dispatch work we cannot thermally protect. That interlock is specified and not yet built.

Gate: Phase 3 (mining) stays blocked until an explicit go-ahead and a cool thermal path. Spurious stop beats missing stop.

What we publish / do not

Publish: case identity, objectives, procedures, pass criteria, per-run status and timings, defects in our own code.

Do not publish: measurements, temperatures, voltages, hashrates, serials, host names, absolute paths, NDA material. The rule is in PUBLISHING.md and enforced by tools/check-public.sh.
